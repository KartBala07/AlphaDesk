# Ten Years of US Markets: 2016–2026

**Data cut-off: 2026-11-15.** The last complete trading day in the underlying data is Friday 2026-11-13; the 2026 figures below are year-to-date, not full-year.

This is a factual chronology of the US equity and crypto markets, written to sit alongside a technical-analysis tool that has no predictive claims of its own. Every return figure in this document is a **price return** — index level change, dividends excluded — unless it is explicitly labelled otherwise. Where a number could not be verified against a primary source it has been left out rather than estimated.

---

## How the numbers were produced

**Conventions**

| Item | Definition used here |
|---|---|
| Return type | Price return (capital change only). No dividends, no total-return series. |
| S&P 500 | Index level, FRED series [`SP500`](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500); annual figures cross-checked against [History of Market's price-return table](https://historyofmarket.com/sp500/annual). |
| Nasdaq-100 | Index level, FRED series [`NASDAQ100`](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQ100), full history from 1986. |
| Nasdaq Composite | Index level, FRED series [`NASDAQCOM`](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM), full history from 1971. |
| Bitcoin | Coinbase BTC-USD daily close, FRED series [`CBBTCUSD`](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CBBTCUSD). Other venues differ by a few tenths of a percent; annual figures here are on the Coinbase close basis. |
| Drawdowns | **Close-to-close**, peak close to trough close. Intraday extremes are larger. |
| Volatility | CBOE VIX close, FRED series [`VIXCLS`](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS). |
| Policy rate | Upper bound of the federal funds target range, FRED series [`DFEDTARU`](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFEDTARU). |

**One limitation up front.** FRED serves the `SP500` series with a rolling ten-year window, so the S&P 500 data used here begins 2016-11-14. The 2016 calendar-year S&P 500 return therefore comes from the History of Market price-return table, which agrees to one decimal place with the FRED-derived returns for every year from 2017 through 2026. That agreement is the reason the mixed sourcing is acceptable; it is not an assumption.

## 2016 — Slow start, two shocks, a post-election re-rating

**Theme:** a low-growth, low-rate grind punctuated by two political shocks and an improving second half.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+9.5%**, Nasdaq-100 **+5.9%**, Nasdaq Composite **+7.5%**, Bitcoin **+126%**.

The year opened with the hangover from the 2015–16 industrial and oil-sector contraction. Equity risk premia widened in the first six weeks; the VIX closed at 28.14 on 2016-02-11, its highest close of the year ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)). From there the market recovered steadily, and the two big event days were political rather than macro: the UK's Brexit vote on 2016-06-23 and the US presidential election on 2016-11-08. The post-election move was a rotation, not a rally in everything — financials and small caps re-rated on expectations of deregulation and lower taxes while bond-proxy sectors lagged.

The policy backdrop was still extraordinarily accommodative. The Federal Reserve raised the target range once, on 2016-12-14, to 0.50–0.75% — only the second hike of the cycle that began in December 2015 ([DFEDTARU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFEDTARU)).

Bitcoin more than doubled, from roughly $430 to $973 on the Coinbase close, an early leg of a move that would become the defining speculative episode of the following year.

**Defining events**
- 2016-02-11 — VIX closes at 28.14, the year's high, during the China/oil-driven risk-off.
- 2016-06-23/24 — UK votes to leave the EU; global equities sell off.
- 2016-11-08 — Trump elected; post-election rotation into financials and small caps.
- 2016-12-14 — Fed hikes to 0.50–0.75%.

## 2017 — The low-volatility melt-up

**Theme:** synchronized global growth, US tax reform, and the quietest volatility regime on record.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+19.4%**, Nasdaq-100 **+31.5%**, Nasdaq Composite **+28.2%**, Bitcoin **+1,324%**.

2017 was the year volatility disappeared. The VIX averaged **11.09** — the lowest annual average in the series' history back to 1990 — and its highest close of the year was 16.04 ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)). With realized volatility near single digits, the S&P 500 compounded steadily and finished the year at records; the Nasdaq-100's +31.5% was its best year since 2009. The Fed raised rates three times (March, June, December) to 1.25–1.50%, and markets treated each hike as a non-event because inflation was still below target.

The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, signed 2017-12-22, cut the corporate rate from 35% to 21% and drove a late-year acceleration in earnings expectations for 2018. Bitcoin went parabolic over the same twelve months — from $973 to $13,863 on the Coinbase close, with an intraday peak near $19,650 on 2017-12-17 — and CME launched bitcoin futures that month, which coincided almost exactly with the top.

**Defining events**
- 2017-03/06/12 — three Fed hikes to 1.25–1.50%.
- 2017-12-17 — Bitcoin's first cycle peak (~$19.7k intraday); CME futures launch.
- 2017-12-22 — Tax Cuts and Jobs Act signed.

## 2018 — Two shocks: volatility in February, a bear market in the fourth quarter

**Theme:** the return of two-way risk — a volatility shock in winter, a policy-driven selloff in autumn.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **−6.2%**, Nasdaq-100 **−1.0%**, Nasdaq Composite **−3.9%**, Bitcoin **−73.4%**.

On 2018-02-05 the VIX closed at **37.32**, up from 17.31 the previous session — a one-day doubling that destroyed the inverse-volatility ETN XIV and forced a reassessment of short-volatility carry across the industry ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)). The equity market recovered through the summer, with the Nasdaq-100 making a record close on 2018-08-29, before the fourth quarter delivered a genuine bear market.

The trigger was a collision of tightening policy and trade conflict. The Fed hiked four times in 2018, taking the target range to 2.25–2.50% by 2018-12-20, and the 10-year yield pushed toward 3.2%. Escalating tariffs between the US and China added an earnings-growth discount. The S&P 500 fell **19.8%** from its 2018-09-20 close to its 2018-12-24 close; the Nasdaq-100 fell **23.0%** from 2018-08-29 to the same trough. The Christmas Eve low marked the bottom, and the market rallied hard into January.

Bitcoin's first full bear cycle played out here: from the December 2017 peak of $19,650 (Coinbase close) to $3,183 on 2018-12-15, a decline of **83.8%**.

**Defining events**
- 2018-02-05 — VIX closes at 37.32; short-volatility products collapse.
- 2018-09-20 → 2018-12-24 — S&P 500 −19.8%; Nasdaq-100 −23.0%.
- 2018-12-20 — fourth Fed hike of the year to 2.25–2.50%.
- 2018-12-15 — Bitcoin bottoms at $3,183, −83.8% from its record.

## 2019 — The pivot, the inversion, and the repo spike

**Theme:** the Fed reverses, and a mid-cycle easing cycle extends the expansion.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+28.9%**, Nasdaq-100 **+38.0%**, Nasdaq Composite **+35.2%**, Bitcoin **+93.9%**.

After the December 2018 selloff, the Fed abandoned its tightening bias in early 2019 and cut rates three times — 2019-08-01, 2019-09-19 and 2019-10-31 — taking the target range from 2.25–2.50% to 1.50–1.75% ([DFEDTARU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFEDTARU)). That pivot, plus a resolution-free but de-escalating trade dispute, produced one of the decade's strongest years: the S&P 500 gained 28.9%, its best year since 2013, and the Nasdaq-100 gained 38.0%.

Two mechanical stresses were worth remembering. On 2019-08-14 the 2-year/10-year Treasury curve inverted for the first time since 2007. Then, on 2019-09-17, overnight dollar funding seized up: SOFR printed **5.25%** against a median of roughly 2.15% that month ([SOFR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SOFR)), forcing the New York Fed into its first large-scale repo operations since the crisis. It was a liquidity plumbing problem, not a solvency one, but it previewed how fragile the post-QE market structure had become.

Bitcoin nearly doubled again, recovering to $7,158 by year-end after touching almost $12,900 in June.

**Defining events**
- 2019-07/09/10 — three Fed cuts to 1.50–1.75%.
- 2019-08-14 — 2s10s curve inverts.
- 2019-09-17 — SOFR spikes to 5.25%; Fed repo intervention begins.
- 2019-12 — S&P 500 and Nasdaq set records into year-end.

## 2020 — The pandemic crash and the fastest recovery on record

**Theme:** an exogenous shock, a policy response of unprecedented speed and size, and a market that repriced within months.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+16.3%**, Nasdaq-100 **+47.6%**, Nasdaq Composite **+43.6%**, Bitcoin **+305%**.

The S&P 500 peaked on 2020-02-19 at 3,386.15 and bottomed on 2020-03-23 at 2,237.40 — a **−33.9%** decline in 33 calendar days, the fastest bear market in the index's history on a close-to-close basis ([SP500](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500)). The VIX closed at **82.69** on 2020-03-16, its highest close ever ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)).

The policy response was immediate and overwhelming. The Fed cut 50bp on 2020-03-03 and then 100bp on 2020-03-15 to a 0–0.25% range, announcing open-ended asset purchases on the same day ([Fed, 2020-03-03](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20200303a.htm); [Fed, 2020-03-15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20200315a.htm)). Congress passed the $2.2 trillion CARES Act on 2020-03-27. The S&P 500 made a new record close on 2020-08-18, less than five months after the trough.

The character of the rally mattered for everything that followed. The Nasdaq-100 gained 47.6% for the year — its best since 2009 — as remote-work and e-commerce names re-rated, while energy and financials lagged badly. Bitcoin, which had fallen 74.7% from its 2017 peak to $4,980 on 2020-03-12, finished the year at $29,027.

**Defining events**
- 2020-02-19 → 2020-03-23 — S&P 500 −33.9%, the fastest bear market on record.
- 2020-03-15 — Fed cuts to zero and launches open-ended QE.
- 2020-03-27 — CARES Act signed ($2.2tn).
- 2020-08-18 — S&P 500 makes a new all-time high.

## 2021 — Liquidity, memes, and the inflation turn

**Theme:** the last year of the liquidity era — retail speculation, record issuance, and the first serious inflation prints.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+26.9%**, Nasdaq-100 **+26.6%**, Nasdaq Composite **+21.4%**, Bitcoin **+59.3%**.

2021 was a year of narrow, violent rotations inside a strong index trend. The January GameStop squeeze forced a broader conversation about retail order flow and short interest; the VIX closed at 37.21 on 2021-01-27 ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)). Unprofitable long-duration growth peaked in February and then de-rated for the rest of the year even as the indices kept rising — a divergence that would resolve violently in 2022.

Inflation was the macro story. CPI moved above 5% year-over-year in the spring (5.30% in June on the seasonally adjusted index) and finished the year at **+7.17%** ([CPIAUCSL](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL)), which forced the Fed to retire "transitory" and begin tapering in November. The Fed did not hike in 2021; the target range stayed at 0–0.25% all year.

Bitcoin made its cycle high on 2021-11-08 at $67,510 on the Coinbase close.

**Defining events**
- 2021-01-27 — GameStop squeeze; VIX closes at 37.21.
- 2021-06 — CPI moves above 5% y/y on the seasonally adjusted index.
- 2021-11-03 — Fed announces tapering.
- 2021-11-08 — Bitcoin's cycle peak at $67,510 (Coinbase close).

## 2022 — The rate-hike bear market

**Theme:** the fastest tightening cycle since the early 1980s repriced every duration-sensitive asset at once.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **−19.4%**, Nasdaq-100 **−33.0%**, Nasdaq Composite **−33.1%**, Bitcoin **−64.3%**.

This was the decade's only calendar-year loss for the S&P 500, and its worst since 2008. The Fed raised rates seven times for a cumulative **425bp**, from 0.25% to 4.50%, including four consecutive 75bp moves — the first of which, on 2022-06-15, was the largest single hike since 1994 ([Fed, 2022-06-15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20220615a.htm); [DFEDTARU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFEDTARU)). Headline CPI peaked at 9.1% year-over-year in June 2022; the seasonally adjusted FRED series peaks at **8.98%** the same month ([CPIAUCSL](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL)).

The equity drawdown was long and two-legged. The S&P 500 fell **25.4%** from its 2022-01-03 close to its 2022-10-12 close. The Nasdaq-100 fell **35.6%** from its 2021-11-19 close to 2022-12-28; its calendar-year loss of 33.0% was its fourth-worst year since 1986, behind only 2008, 2002 and 2000. The damage was concentrated in the assets that had benefited most from zero rates: unprofitable software, SPACs, and crypto.

Crypto's second bear cycle was also its most destructive in terms of institutional damage. Bitcoin fell from $67,510 to $15,756 on 2022-11-21, a **−76.7%** drawdown, and the collapse of Terra/Luna in May and of FTX in November destroyed two of the industry's largest franchises in a single year.

Outside the US, the September 2022 UK gilt crisis — forced selling by liability-driven investment funds — was the clearest demonstration of how quickly a rate shock can become a margin call in a leveraged system.

**Defining events**
- 2022-01-03 → 2022-10-12 — S&P 500 −25.4%.
- 2022-06-15 — first 75bp Fed hike since 1994; CPI peaks at 9.1% y/y.
- 2022-09 — UK gilt/LDI crisis.
- 2022-11-11 — FTX files for bankruptcy; Bitcoin bottoms at $15,756 two weeks later.

## 2023 — The AI re-rating

**Theme:** a narrow, earnings-backed technology rally that widened into a broad advance in the fourth quarter.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+24.2%**, Nasdaq-100 **+53.8%**, Nasdaq Composite **+43.4%**, Bitcoin **+156%**.

2023 began with the regional-bank crisis: Silicon Valley Bank was closed by the FDIC on 2023-03-10 ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23017.html)), Signature Bank followed two days later, and Credit Suisse was absorbed by UBS over the weekend of 2023-03-18/19. The S&P 500's 2023 low close came on 2023-01-05 at 3,808, before any of it.

The recovery was driven by AI infrastructure demand. The Nasdaq-100 gained **53.8%**, its best year since 2009 and its fourth-best since 1986, with the gains extremely concentrated in a handful of mega-cap semiconductor and platform companies for the first three quarters. The Fed delivered its last hike of the cycle on 2023-07-26, taking the range to 5.25–5.50%, then held. The 10-year yield nevertheless pushed to 5% in October, and the S&P 500 gave back 10.3% between 2023-07-31 and 2023-10-27 before the November–December melt-up.

Bitcoin recovered 156% to $42,301, the first leg of the 2024 cycle.

**Defining events**
- 2023-03-10 — SVB fails; regional-bank crisis.
- 2023-07-26 — final Fed hike of the cycle to 5.25–5.50%.
- 2023-10 — 10-year yield touches 5%; S&P 500 −10.3% peak-to-trough.
- 2023-11/12 — AI-led year-end rally; Nasdaq-100 +53.8% for the year.

## 2024 — AI capex and the start of the easing cycle

**Theme:** the AI trade broadened from chips into power, cooling and software, while the Fed finally began cutting.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+23.3%**, Nasdaq-100 **+24.9%**, Nasdaq Composite **+28.6%**, Bitcoin **+123%**.

The year's defining feature was capital expenditure. Hyperscaler spending guidance escalated every quarter, and the market rewarded anything with AI-related revenue exposure. The S&P 500 made **57 record closes** over the year and gained 23.31% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/02/cnbc-daily-open-sp-soared-in-2024-despite-a-weak-fourth-quarter.html)).

Volatility returned twice. On 2024-08-05 the unwinding of the yen carry trade produced a global risk-off day; the VIX closed at **38.57**, its highest close since March 2020 ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)). The S&P 500 fell 8.5% from its 2024-07-16 close to its 2024-08-05 close. Then, after the Fed's 50bp cut on 2024-09-18 ([Fed, 2024-09-18](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20240918a.htm)), a hawkish December cut briefly revived the tightening scare.

Bitcoin's fourth cycle was driven by institutional access: the SEC approved spot bitcoin exchange-traded products on 2024-01-10 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/01/10/sec-approves-rule-changes-that-pave-the-way-for-bitcoin-etfs.html)), the April halving cut issuance, and the price ran from $42,301 to $94,384 by year-end, a gain of **123%**.

**Defining events**
- 2024-01-10 — SEC approves spot bitcoin ETPs.
- 2024-08-05 — yen carry unwind; VIX closes at 38.57.
- 2024-09-18 — Fed begins cutting with a 50bp move.
- 2024-11-05 — US election; equities rally into year-end.

## 2025 — Tariffs, then a melt-up

**Theme:** a policy shock in April, a V-shaped recovery, and a third consecutive double-digit year that almost nobody forecast in the spring.

**Price returns:** S&P 500 **+16.4%**, Nasdaq-100 **+20.2%**, Nasdaq Composite **+20.4%**, Bitcoin **−7.1%**.

The year's first quarter was dominated by tariffs. After initial duties on Canada, Mexico and China in February, the 2025-04-02 "Liberation Day" announcement imposed a 10% baseline tariff plus country-specific reciprocal rates. Between 2025-04-02 and the 2025-04-07 intraday low of 4,835.04, the S&P 500 fell 14.7%; on a close-to-close basis the drawdown from the 2025-02-19 peak to the 2025-04-08 trough was **−18.9%** (Nasdaq-100 **−22.9%**). The VIX closed at 52.33 on 2025-04-08, its highest since 2020 ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)).

The reversal was as violent as the decline. On 2025-04-09, after the administration paused the reciprocal tariffs for 90 days, the S&P 500 rose **9.5%** — its third-largest single-day gain since the Second World War — and the Nasdaq Composite rose 12.2%, its second-best day ever ([Briefing.com 2025 review](https://www.briefing.com/the-big-picture/archive/2025/12/31/2025-year-in-review)). From the April low to year-end the S&P 500 gained roughly 42%.

The rest of the year was a liquidity-and-AI story. The Fed cut three times — September, October and December — taking the range from 4.25–4.50% to 3.50–3.75% ([DFEDTARU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFEDTARU)). A 43-day government shutdown, the longest in US history, ran from 2025-10-01 to 2025-11-12 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/longest-us-government-shutdowns-2025-10-01)). Gold broke $4,000/oz for the first time on 2025-10-08 and set a record close of $4,356.30 on 2025-10-20 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/gold-vaults-over-4000-rush-safety-fed-easing-bets-2025-10-08/)). Nvidia became the first company to reach a $5 trillion market capitalization in late October.

Crypto diverged. Bitcoin's record close of **$124,720** came on 2025-10-05, and five days later a leveraged liquidation cascade removed roughly $19 billion of positions in hours ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/06/from-retail-leverage-to-etf-flows-a-year-after-its-record-high-bitcoin-is-down-just-32)). Bitcoin finished the year at $87,696, down 7.1% — the first down year since 2022 and the only major asset in this table to lose money in 2025.

**Defining events**
- 2025-04-02 — "Liberation Day" tariffs; S&P 500 −14.7% to the 2025-04-07 low.
- 2025-04-09 — 90-day pause; S&P 500 +9.5% in one session.
- 2025-10-05/10 — Bitcoin's record close ($124,720) and the $19bn liquidation cascade.
- 2025-10-01 → 2025-11-12 — 43-day government shutdown; gold above $4,000.

## 2026 — An oil shock, a new Fed chair, and the AI unwind

**Theme:** a Middle East war that repriced energy, a Fed that had to hike again, and an autumn AI-capex de-rating that turned into a political event.

**Price returns, year to date through 2026-11-13:** S&P 500 **+10.5%**, Nasdaq-100 **+10.7%**, Nasdaq Composite **+8.2%**, Bitcoin **−28.3%**.

The year started at records and then broke. The S&P 500 peaked at 6,979 on 2026-01-27 and fell **9.1%** to 6,344 on 2026-03-30; the Nasdaq-100 fell **11.8%** and the Nasdaq Composite **12.8%** over the same window. The cause was oil: on 2026-02-28 the United States and Israel opened a war against Iran ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)), and by late March a near-total blockade of the Strait of Hormuz — the waterway through which about a fifth of the world's crude normally transits — had pushed energy prices sharply higher. Brent posted its largest monthly percentage gain on record in March, up more than 60%, and US unleaded gasoline hit $4/gallon ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/trump-2026-losses-stocks-iran-battle-rcna266009)). The Nasdaq Composite confirmed a correction on 2026-03-26; the VIX closed at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, the year's high ([VIXCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS)). The S&P 500 lost 5.1% in March and 4.6% in the first quarter.

The recovery was faster than the decline. On 2026-04-07/08, less than two hours before a deadline to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the US agreed a two-week ceasefire with Iran ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-defiant-eve-trumps-ceasefire-deadline-2026-04-07/); [Reuters explainer](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/us-iran-ceasefire-what-we-know-2026-04-08)). Iran then said the Strait was open again to commercial tankers, Brent dropped back below $100, and equities rallied — the S&P 500 rose from 6,528 on 2026-03-31 to 7,580 by 2026-05-29, setting records in late April as oil eased and first-quarter earnings beat ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-trump-oil-iran-e1c194b5266c4eb58dc993cc4a9f9b50)). A US naval blockade of Iran followed on 2026-04-13 after talks in Islamabad failed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_naval_blockade_of_Iran)). The S&P 500 went on to a record close of **7,818.93 on 2026-10-06**.

Policy turned hawkish. Kevin Warsh was sworn in as Fed chair on 2026-05-22 ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20260522a.htm)), and with CPI re-accelerating to 3.7% year-over-year by September the FOMC **raised** the target range 25bp on 2026-09-17 to 3.75–4.00%, the first hike since 2023, then held in October ([DFEDTARU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFEDTARU); [CPIAUCSL](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL)).

October brought the AI reckoning. Hyperscaler capital-expenditure commitments for the year climbed above $740 billion, and investors began to question the payback; OpenAI cut the target on its latest fundraising round to $20 billion from $30 billion; roughly $1 trillion of market value was erased in the last week of October ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/cracks-are-emerging-in-the-ai-spending-boom)). Then politics landed on top: Democrats swept both chambers in the 2026-11-03 midterms ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/3fec1b0a85a0/legal/government/democrats-vow-rein-trump-he-shows-off-white-house-ballroom-2026-11-05/)). The market's initial reaction was positive — the S&P 500 rose 1.6% on 2026-11-04 — and then reversed violently: on 2026-11-05 the S&P 500 fell **4.36%** to 7,353.73, its largest one-day decline since 2025-04-04, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% into correction territory, the Dow fell 1,833 points, and the Magnificent 7 lost about $1 trillion in market value ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-selloff-ai-fears.html); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/2026110523262/sp-500-falls-436-to-735373-data-talk)). The Nasdaq-100 fell **14.7%** from its 2026-10-06 record to its 2026-11-05 close.

As of 2026-11-13 the S&P 500 sits at 7,561.67, 3.3% below its record, up 10.5% year to date.

Crypto spent the year in a bear market that was unusually shallow by its own history. Bitcoin's low close was $58,586 on 2026-06-30, **−53.0%** from the October 2025 record — against declines of 84% and 77% in the two previous cycles — before recovering to the high $80,000s in September and then fading to $62,891 by 2026-11-14, down 49.6% from the peak and 28.3% year to date.

**Defining events**
- 2026-02-28 — US–Israel war with Iran begins; Hormuz disruption.
- 2026-03-26/30 — Nasdaq correction; S&P 500 bottoms at 6,344 (−9.1% from January).
- 2026-09-17 — Fed hikes 25bp to 3.75–4.00% under new chair Kevin Warsh.
- 2026-11-05 — AI-led rout after the Democratic midterm sweep: S&P 500 −4.36%, Nasdaq −5.5%.

## What this document does not claim

1. **No dividend adjustment.** All returns are price returns. Total returns are roughly 1.5–2.0 percentage points per year higher for the S&P 500 over this period, and the gap matters when judging a timing strategy that spends time in cash.
2. **Close-to-close drawdowns understate intraday damage.** The 2025 and 2026 episodes in particular had intraday lows well below the closing troughs.
3. **Index composition changes are not adjusted for.** The Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500 of 2026 are not the same baskets as in 2016; the returns are index-level, which is what an investor in the index would have earned, but they embed survivorship and rebalancing effects.
4. **2026 is partial.** The year-to-date figures cover 2026-01-01 to 2026-11-13 and should not be annualised.
5. **Bitcoin's venue basis matters.** Coinbase closes differ from other exchanges by a few tenths of a percent, and intraday records differ more. The cycle dates and percentages here are on one consistent basis; other publications quoting $126,080 as the record high are using intraday prints.
